## Interpretation

- **HNSW gives little or no speed-up at our real sizes.** Per camera, HNSW at ef 200 beats NumPy brute force only on the five larger appearance indexes (19.8k–41.6k rows: 0.9–1.2 ms vs 2.6–3.8 ms); on appearance/G474 (6.6k rows) and on every action index (7.2k–7.6k rows, ~0.6 ms vs ~0.2 ms) NumPy is faster. The API searches the 6 per-camera indexes of a branch in turn: merged appearance (175,039 rows) 13.7 ms at ef 200 (recall 0.939) vs 18.6 ms for exact NumPy over all rows; merged action (44,682 rows) 9.5 ms (recall 0.943) vs 7.0 ms for NumPy — **slower than NumPy, and approximate**. The engine's own exact scan is slower than NumPy everywhere (scalar dot loop vs multi-threaded BLAS).
- **Per-camera vs one index.** One HNSW over all cameras is ~9× faster for appearance (1.47 ms at ef 200) but recall drops to 0.781 (0.956 at ef 800); action 0.81 ms, recall 0.748. The per-camera layout trades speed for recall and exact camera filtering. This was the 7.1 decision; at these sizes every option is well under the 46 ms end-to-end API latency (7.3), so the numbers do not force a change.
- **Time filter.** Recall vs exact filtered search rounds to 1.000 in both branches (worst window 0.996 appearance, 1.000 action) because most 30-min windows go to an exact path: appearance 1,098 exact_small + 2,448 exact_fallback + 114 hnsw_filtered; action 3,050 + 572 + 38. HNSW-then-filter rarely returns enough in-range hits. Appearance filtered median 3.50 ms vs 0.65 ms for exact over the window: for narrow windows, going exact directly would be faster. Not changed (out of step scope); candidate improvement.
- **ef_search.** Config stays at 200 (the pre-set value; no pre-registered rule for changing it). At ef 800 the merged branches reach recall 0.995 (appearance, 26.4 ms) and 0.993 (action, 16.2 ms). For Ashutosh to decide at 8.1: ef 800, or exact NumPy, both fit the latency budget. Lowest per-camera recall at ef 200: action/G340 0.835 (0.936 at ef 800).
- **Synthetic 1M / 2M (random unit vectors) say nothing about recall on real data.** Random 512-d points have no cluster structure, so graph routing barely works: recall@200 at ef 200 is 0.020 (1M) and 0.012 (2M), at most 0.072 at ef 800. They do show cost: build 69 min (1M) and 147 min (2M), peak RSS 4.1 / 7.9 GiB, exact search growing linearly (engine 252 → 488 ms, NumPy 106 → 201 ms) while HNSW latency stays ~3–12 ms. Not verified: whether structured 1M+ data (e.g. real CLIP embeddings) keeps the recall seen at real sizes. The 1-min load average recorded at the end of the 2M run was 11.0, so those latencies are less clean than the real-size ones.
